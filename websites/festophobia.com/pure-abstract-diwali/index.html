So, What’s Diwali Afterall?

  • The word Diwali originates from Deepawali (festival of lights).
  • It is based on an old mythological scripture called “Ramayan”.
  • The protagonist of the story is “Lord Ram”, who is the eldest prince of kingdom “Ayodhya” .
  • Lord Ram was on exile for 14 years along with his brother “Laxman”, and wife “Sita”.
  • Diwali was first celebrated on return of Lord Ram to Ayodhya from exile and is celebrated every year since.
  • Festival signifies victory of good over evil, light over dark.
  • Thus, darkness of night is tried to be illuminated with light during the whole festival.

Celebration

  • The celebration is carried over for 5 days, which are declared national holidays.
  • People socialize by visiting places of their relatives.
  • The served fare are usually sweets.
  • Deities that are worshipped – Kuber, Ganesha, Saraswati, Laxmi, Ram, Krishna (Goverdhan Puja).
  • Kids get their share of stimulation by firing crackers in gatherings.
  • Festival is a boom over economy, so sellers try to lure with a great deal of offers.

The 5 Diwali days

It’s interesting to note that exile was over just after the final battle between Lord Ram and antagonist “Raavan” was over.

The first day of Diwali Dhanteras falls 18 days after “Dussehara”(Defeat of ten headed i.e. Raavan).

  • Dhanteras :

Dhan-Teras (Dhan = wealth, Teras = 13th day of month), this day is thought to be lucky in terms of wealth and goddess of wealth, Laxmi is worshipped over, moreover major shopping during the whole festival takes place on this day.

  • Naraka Chaturdashi :

Also called Choti-Diwali, all the preparations and decorations are held on this day, be it house decorations, rangoli making, applying henna on women’s hands or making home-made sweets

  • Diwali :

The main festival day, people wear their best or newest clothes, meet and gift their acquaintances, eat lots of sweets with whole heart and fire crackers. It is believed that Laxmi roams over earth on this day, so another Laxmi Puja is held on this day and all the doors are kept open for her welcoming.

  • Diwali Padwa/ Goverdhan pooja:

Diwali padwa is not much appreciated, it is celebrated in very few regions of the country. This day is devoted to the relationship between husband and wife. It’s their day to show mutual love and respect by gifts and special food. Goverdhan pooja is also performed the day after Diwali. Goverdhan pooja is derived from a different mythology, of Lord Krishna’s. The pooja is performed to worship mount Goverdhan, which according to lore Krishna lifted with just his pinky finger to provide shelter from flood and heavy rain.

  • Bhau-Beej:

Also known as bhai dooj, this day focuses on the relationship of brother and sister. Moreover it’s also known as sister’s day. As brother’s already have their day on Rakshabandhan.
