    Dhanteras : First day of Diwali

    by Saransh Karira in Diwali 2015 

    Dhanteras from sanskrit word Dhantrayodashi ( Dhan = wealth + Trayodashi = 13th day of month,as per hindu calendar) is the first day among five days of diwali. The festival is focused over dieties related with wealth such as Lord Kubera and Goddess Laxmi.Purchases of assets and precious metals such as gold, silver and platinum hold a huge significance on this day and thus day is very crucial for business. Every other product is on an offer or sale to lure the customers, the marketplaces are decorated and lightened up.

    Celebration :

    The day starts with the worshipping of Goddess Laxmi for prosperity and well-being, Lord Kubera for wealth and assets and also precious metals as they hold pious significance.Lamps are lit as darkness approaches during evening to welcome Dhan-Laxmi, beautifully coloured patterns are made over floors with dry powdered colours called “Rangoli” along with the footprints of Laxmi using rice flour and vermilion powder. Females of house decorate their hands with patterns of “Henna“.Hymns and Artis are sung devoted to Laxmi and offerings of fruits and sweets like Rabri, Laddoo, Shrikhand, Kulfi, Barfi, Jalebi, misri mawa are done and eaten as well. Families visit to jewellers to buy gold or silver jewellery and utensils and many also wear jewellery during the day.Tiny clay lamps called “Diyas” are kept lit all over the night. In Maharashtra, crushed dry coriander seeds along with jaggery is offered to Goddess Laxmi. The offerings are called “Naivedya”. In villages, Cattles and farms are worshipped as they are the main source of income.

    Lore :

    It is believed that during the churning of ocean for nectar by gods and demons, Dhanvantari, “The Doctor of Gods” emerged out with a jar of elixir in hand on this day. Another legend says about the 16-year-old son of King Hima. He was destined to death on third day of his marriage by snake’s bite. His newly wed wife on this day surrounded their bedroom chamber with all the gold and silver jewellery and lit the lamps. When Yamraj, God of Death came in snake’s disguise, his eyes were dazzled with all the shine and light around. Snake sat there for the night listening to the songs and stories wife spoke in order to keep the prince awake. The night passed and morning came over, snake moved on and the prince was saved. Thus the next day is called Naraka Chaturdashi (Naraka = Hell, Chaturdashi = 14th day of the month) to honor King Yama/Yamaraj.As this day falls just before Diwali, it is also called Choti Diwali or Minor Diwali.

     

    Dhanteras is then succeeded by second day of diwali i.e. Kali chaudas also called Naraka chaturdashi
