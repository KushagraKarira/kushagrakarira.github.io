Naraka Chaturdashi : Second day of Diwali

by Saransh Karira · October 22, 2015

MahaKali’s victory over Demon Narakasura

Naraka Chaturdashi, the second day of Diwali, falls on fourteenth day of hindu lunar month (waning moon),hence the name Chaturdashi, this day is also called choti Diwali as it falls before Diwali day. It falls after Dhanteras, 13th day of diwali.According to the legend Demon Narakasura was killed by Lord Krishna and Kali on this day.This day is also called Kali Chaudas ( Kali meaning Dark or eternal and Chaudas ,the fourteenth day of hindu month Kartik).In some regions of India this day is meant to Worship MahaKali or Shakti, who killed demon Narakasur, This day conveys to abolish laziness and evil which create hell in our lives.

Rituals :

Rituals are held during early morning, the rest of usual festivities are carried on as the day passes by.The rituals of Kali Chaudas strongly suggest the roots of Diwali as a harvest festival.A pooja/prayer is performed with flowers, sandalwood and oil. A prasad of Ghee, jaggery, coconuts, sesame seeds and poha(pounded rice flakes) are offered to Lord Hanuman. Different varieties of gourmets are made from poha as servings, The rice for this poha is taken freshly from the harvest that time in both Urban and rural areas, mainly in western India.

All the decorations such as home decoration, rangoli making, mehndi on hands etc are carried over this day.

On this day, women prefer head wash and application of “Kajal” to keep Kali nazar/evil-eye away. Men prefer to rub themselves with perfumed oils before bath.After bath, clean or new clothes are worn. Some people like Pujabis and Sindhis offer food to their forefathers on this day. Others worship their Kul Devi which is local to their place of origin and offer her Nivet. Huge gatherings of friends and relatives are held for breakfast. sweets like laddoo, rabri, misri mawa, kulfi,  jalebi, shrikhand are served over the day, especially during mid-day meal. Greetings are exchanged. The evening sky is lightened up with the firing of crackers which spreads an aura of joy among the kids and houses are lightened up with oil lamps.

In Tamil Nadu, Main Diwali is celebrated on Kali Chaudas, i.e fourteenth day of month, one day before the rest of India. Usually rituals follow same timelines i.e. oil baths, pooja and celebrations are performed along with Laxmi Pooja on this day, while crackers are lit on the fifteenth day.

In some regions like Goa, paper-made Narakasura filled with crackers and dry grass signifying evil is burnt in early morning followed by firing of crackers. People return home to bath with perfumed oils to spiritually cleanse themselves. Women perform aarti of their men.
